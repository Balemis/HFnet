Lightweight and Low-Latency Dual Channel Speech Enhancement via Mel-Domain Modeling and Asymmetric Windows
Online Supplement

Abstract

To address the strict computational and latency constraints of edge devices, we propose HFNet, a lightweight dual-channel speech enhancement framework. HFNet integrates an Aux-IVA spatial front-end with Mel-domain neural modeling to drastically reduce spectral redundancy. Furthermore, an asymmetric window configuration preserves robust spectral resolution while strictly restricting algorithmic latency to 5~ms. Experiments show HFNet-Lite (23.24K parameters, 42.57 M/s MACs) and HFNet-Base (85.86K parameters, 290.95 M/s MACs) consistently outperform existing models under different SNR conditions, while maintaining a Real-Time Factor (RTF) well below 1.0, proving their readiness for real-time edge deployment.

Datasets

The simulated dual-channel datasets are constructed using the image source method. Room impulse responses (RIRs) are generated for a linear two-microphone array with 4 cm spacing. The training samples are generated by convolving clean single-channel speech signals with the simulated RIRs. The clean speech is drawn from the VCTK corpus which contains 11,572 training utterances and 824 test utterances. The room size is randomly sampled from $3\ \mathrm{m} \times 3\ \mathrm{m} \times 2.5\ \mathrm{m}$ to $10\ \mathrm{m} \times 10\ \mathrm{m} \times 3\ \mathrm{m}$, and the reverberation time $RT_{60}$ ranges from 0.1 s to 0.4 s. The distance between the source and the microphone array is uniformly selected between 0.5 m and 3 m, while the direction-of-arrival (DOA) difference between the target speech and the interfering noise is constrained to be greater than $5^\circ$. We convolve the clean speech signals with the generated dual-channel RIRs to obtain simulated noisy speech. The early reflections (within the first 50 ms) of the first channel are preserved as the training target. To ensure acoustic diversity, diverse stationary and highly non-stationary noises are drawn from DNS-3 dataset. During training, the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) is randomly sampled from $-5$ dB to 15 dB. The validation set follows the same SNR range and contain 500 noisy-clean pairs. For testing, we adopt the original test splits of VCTK (824 utterances). The model is evaluated under three SNR conditions ($-5$ dB, $0$ dB, and $5$ dB).
