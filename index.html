HFNet: Low-Latency Dual-Channel Speech Enhancement via Mel-Domain Modeling
Online Supplement

Authors

Xiang Ye, Wenbin Jiang, Jiatao Yan, JingRui Qian, and Axiu Mao

Abstract

Dual-channel speech enhancement for edge devices requires an effective balance among model performance, computational efficiency, and algorithmic latency. This letter presents HFNet, a highly efficient framework that achieves robust dual-channel speech enhancement with only 5~ms latency. The proposed framework decouples spatial processing from spectral refinement by combining Aux-IVA spatial priors with Mel-domain neural modeling, thereby reducing the spectral redundancy inherent in high-resolution time--frequency representations. Moreover, an asymmetric windowing strategy preserves the spectral resolution required for spatial filtering while maintaining minimal reconstruction latency. Experimental results on both speech enhancement and speech recognition demonstrate that HFNet consistently outperforms advanced baselines while maintaining low computational complexity, with HFNet-Lite (22.84~K parameters, 50.41~M MACs/s) and HFNet-Base (84.58~K parameters, 292.70~M MACs/s) requiring only a fraction of the computational resources of competing methods.

Datasets

The simulated dual-channel datasets are generated using the image-source method. RIRs are simulated for a linear two-microphone array with 4~cm spacing. Clean speech is drawn from the VCTK corpus, comprising 11572 training utterances and 824 test utterances, while noises are selected from the DNS-3 dataset. Room dimensions are randomly sampled from $3\times3\times2.5~\mathrm{m}^3$ to $10\times10\times3~\mathrm{m}^3$, with reverberation times ($RT_{60}$) ranging from 0.1~s to 0.4~s. The source-to-array distance is uniformly sampled between 0.5~m and 3~m, and the direction-of-arrival (DOA) difference between the target speech and interfering noise is constrained to exceed $5^\circ$. Simulated noisy speech is generated by convolving clean speech with the dual-channel RIRs, and the early reflections (within the first 50~ms) of the first channel are used as the training target. During training, the SNR is uniformly sampled from -5 to 15dB. The validation set follows the same SNR range and contains 500 noisy-clean pairs. For testing, the original VCTK test split (824 utterances) is used, and evaluation is conducted at SNRs of -5, 0, and 5dB.

For blind testing, the dataset is generated using the same procedure as above, except that 500 utterances from the LibriSpeech test_clean subset are used as the clean speech. The SNR is fixed at 0dB, yielding 500 noisy-clean pairs.
