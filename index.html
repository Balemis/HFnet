Lightweight and Low-Latency Dual Channel Speech Enhancement via Mel-Domain Modeling and Asymmetric Windows
Online Supplement

Authors

Xiang Ye, Wenbin Jiang, Jiatao Yan, JingRui Qian, and Axiu Mao

Abstract

Dual-channel speech enhancement for edge devices demands an effective trade-off among enhancement performance, computational efficiency, and algorithmic latency. In this letter, we propose HFNet, a highly efficient framework that achieves robust dual-channel speech enhancement with only a 5 ms latency. The proposed approach decouples spatial processing from spectral refinement by integrating an Aux-IVA spatial front-end with a Mel-domain neural network, significantly reducing the spectral redundancy introduced by high-resolution time-frequency representations. Furthermore, an asymmetric windowing strategy maintains the spectral resolution required for accurate spatial filtering without increasing reconstruction latency. Experimental results demonstrate that HFNet-Lite (22.84 K parameters, 50.41 M MACs/s) and HFNet-Base (84.58 K parameters, 292.70 M MACs/s) consistently outperform advanced baselines across a comprehensive set of objective speech enhancement metrics while maintaining substantially lower computational complexity.

Datasets

The simulated dual-channel datasets are constructed using the image source method~\cite{allen1979image}. Room impulse responses (RIRs) are generated for a linear two-microphone array with 4 cm spacing. The clean speech is drawn from the VCTK corpus~\cite{botinhao2016investigating} which contains 11{,}572 training utterances and 824 test utterances. Noises are taken from the DNS-3 dataset~\cite{reddy2021interspeech}. The training samples are generated by convolving clean single-channel speech signals with the simulated RIRs. The room size is randomly sampled from $3~\mathrm{m} \times 3~\mathrm{m} \times 2.5~\mathrm{m}$ to $10~\mathrm{m} \times 10~\mathrm{m} \times 3~\mathrm{m}$, and the reverberation time $RT_{60}$ ranges from 0.1~s to 0.4~s. The distance between the source and the microphone array is uniformly selected between 0.5~m and 3~m, while the direction-of-arrival (DOA) difference between the target speech and the interfering noise is constrained to be greater than $5^\circ$. We convolve the clean speech signals with the generated dual-channel RIRs to obtain simulated noisy speech. The early reflections (within the first 50~ms) of the first channel are preserved as the training target. During training, the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) is randomly sampled from $-5\sim 15$~dB. The validation set follows the same SNR range and contain 500 noisy--clean pairs. For testing, we adopt the original test splits of VCTK (824 utterances). The model is also evaluated under three SNR conditions (-5~dB, 0~dB, and 5~dB). For the blind test, the dataset is generated in the same way as described above, but the clean speech consists of 500 utterances from the LibriSpeech test\_clean subset~\cite{Librispeech}. The mixing SNR is fixed at -5~dB, each with 500 noisy-clean pairs.
